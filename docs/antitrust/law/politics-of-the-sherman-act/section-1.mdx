# Section 1

**The continuum of political theories about Section 1:**

<u>Libertarian:</u> don't impinge upon people's ability to make agreements with each other
- Founded on rights, not economics
- Counterargument: externalities, people not included in a contract are affected by the contract, some effects are quite harmful and should be regulated (e.g., pollution)
- Libertarian response: in the context of antitrust, selling at a higher price doesn't create an externality because people can choose whether to deal at the higher price

<u>Populist right:</u> unclear what they believe, writings of Josh Hawley are the best source

<u>Center right:</u> don't eliminate Section 1, but keep it simple
- To win a case, a plaintiff must prove actual effects, not likely effects

<u>Center left:</u> this is where the current approach to Section 1 lands

<u>Progressive left (New Brandeis):</u> an expanded version of the center left that increases the burden of proof for defendants

<u>Maxian left:</u> allow consolidation of firms, labor can only coordinate if firms do first
